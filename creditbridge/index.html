How it works

Verified facts from the IRS and Congressional Research Service — nothing more.

1

Give cash to an eligible SGO

On or after Jan 1, 2027, donate cash to a Scholarship Granting Organization on a participating state’s IRS list. Designate it as a qualified contribution.

2

Claim a dollar-for-dollar credit

Individuals may claim a nonrefundable federal credit equal to the gift, up to $1,700 per taxpayer. It reduces tax owed — it is not a cash refund by itself.

3

Donate from anywhere

You can generally give from any state. Scholarships go to eligible students in opted-in states. Living in a non-participating state does not by itself block the donor credit.

4

Watch the offsets

The federal credit is reduced by any state tax credit for the same gift. You cannot also claim a charitable deduction for the same contribution.

FAQ

Straight answers, with sources noted.

Is the credit refundable?

No. It is nonrefundable. It can reduce federal income tax you owe, but will not create a refund by itself if the credit exceeds your tax. Unused amounts may generally be carried forward up to five years. (CRS R48724; IRC §25F.)

Can married couples claim $3,400?

Uncertain until final regs. The statute caps the credit at $1,700 per taxpayer. Proposed Treasury/IRS regulations (Oct 2, 2026, REG-117199-25) interpret each spouse on a joint return as having a separate $1,700 limit — up to $3,400 total — if each spouse makes up to $1,700 of qualifying contributions. Treat $3,400 as proposed, not final.

Do I have to live in an opted-in state?

No for donors. Residents of nonparticipating states may still get credits for gifts to SGOs in participating states. Families in nonparticipating states generally cannot receive these federally supported scholarships.

What counts as a qualifying gift?

Cash contributions only (not stock or other property under the enacted law). Must go to a qualifying SGO on a participating state’s list. Cannot be earmarked for a particular student.

Which states have opted in?

As of September 14, 2026, the IRS lists 30 states with advance elections for 2027: AL, AK, AR, CO, FL, GA, ID, IN, IA, KS, KY, LA, MS, MO, MT, NE, NV, NH, ND, NC, OH, OK, SC, SD, TN, TX, UT, VA, WV, WY.
